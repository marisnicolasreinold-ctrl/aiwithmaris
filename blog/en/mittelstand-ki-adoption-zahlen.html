94 percent without AI: what a current figure reveals about the Mittelstand

16 July 2026 · 2 min read · Maris Reinold

A figure from a recent report on the German Mittelstand is making the rounds: around 94 percent of companies don't yet use AI productively — fewer than six percent run live AI systems. As a headline that works as “Germany is sleeping through AI”. I read it differently.

The trend points up — just slowly

Because the same surveys show movement: the share of firms using generative AI or firmly planning to has risen from around a quarter in 2024 to over half in 2026. So the interest is there. What's missing is the step from trying out to productive use. That gap is exactly the opportunity: whoever takes it now isn't late but ahead.

Why so many get stuck

The reports name surprisingly concrete reasons — and they're not the ones you'd expect:

  • Data. Around 70 percent of industrial companies name data problems as the biggest hurdle. The AI isn't the problem, the chaos underneath is.
  • Missing know-how. About 40 percent can't find AI-qualified people. Building knowledge in your own team often beats hunting for unicorns.
  • No plan after the pilot. Forecasts expect that by the end of 2026 almost a third of AI projects will be shut down again after the pilot phase — mostly due to unclear value or missing scaling.

The lesson for you

These reasons aren't coincidence — they're avoidable. Whoever orders the data base before the grand move, starts small with clear, measurable value, and knows from the outset how things continue after the pilot, doesn't land in the third that quit. That's not rocket science but sequence.

That 94 percent aren't there yet means above all one thing: the head start is still up for grabs. Not with a giant project, but with a first case that works.
