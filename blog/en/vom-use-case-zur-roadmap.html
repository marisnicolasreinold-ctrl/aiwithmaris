Starting small isn't thinking small: from the first use case to an AI roadmap

2 July 2026 · 2 min read · Maris Reinold

“If we do this, let's do it properly.” Behind that sentence often hides a thinking error. You imagine: first the big AI strategy, then the rollout across all departments, then success. In practice the opposite usually happens — the undertaking grows so big it never starts.

Starting small doesn't mean thinking small. It means reaching the big picture through many small, proven steps — instead of through a leap that's too far.

Why the first use case decides everything

The first case isn't the most important because it saves the most. But because it proves something: that AI works in your company, that the team comes along, that idea turns into result. That proof is the currency you fund the second step with — in budget and in trust. A failed giant rollout costs both. A successful small case creates both.

From point to line

A roadmap isn't drawn at the desk but grows out of the first step. Once you've eased accounting, you suddenly see that the same receipt data helps purchasing too. Once the AI agent runs in the inbox, the step to a draft quote is small. One use case opens the door to the next — and the order follows from what you learn along the way, not from a slide from a year ago.

How to build the roadmap without losing focus

  • One after another. Only once a case runs and shows value does the next one come. Starting five projects in parallel means five half-done things.
  • Each step pays off on its own. No use case may prove its worth only “later, once everything's in place”. Each has to carry itself.
  • Build on what's already there. The next case best uses data and processes the first one already opened up.
  • Keep the line in mind, not in stone. You need a direction, not a 50-page strategy. The direction may sharpen with every step.

The honest take

A roadmap isn't a promise that everything works. Some steps lead to a dead end — that's part of it too. The advantage of starting small: a dead end costs you weeks, not an annual budget. You correct early and cheaply instead of late and expensively.

Big AI results in smaller companies rarely come from the one grand move. They come from many small moves that land — and add up. If you want to bring your first steps into a line, that's exactly the stuff of a short conversation. More in the AI guide or directly via contact.
