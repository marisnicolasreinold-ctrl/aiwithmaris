Fear of AI in the team: how to bring people along instead of alienating them

29 July 2026 · 2 min read · Maris Reinold

When you introduce AI in your business, one question is almost always in the room — even if nobody says it out loud: “Will the AI take my job in the end?” Ignore that fear and you won't get open resistance. You'll get something worse: quiet non-participation. And that topples any project.

Take the fear seriously

The worry isn't stupid, it's understandable. The headlines have been telling stories of vanishing jobs for months. Waving it away with “oh, it won't be that bad” signals mainly one thing: I don't take you seriously. So the first step is to name it openly instead of sitting it out.

Relieve, don't replace

The most honest and usually accurate frame: in smaller firms AI rarely takes people away — there aren't enough hands anyway. It takes the routine away. The tedious retyping, the gathering, the standard reply for the tenth time. Show concretely which annoying task disappears — not which position.

Involve, don't impose

People fear what's decided over their heads. So let the team help decide: which annoying chore should go first? Whoever helps pick and try the first case experiences AI as a tool in their own hand — not a threat from above. The affected become the involved.

Honest about change

For all the reassurance: work does change, that's true, and it shouldn't be sugarcoated. Some tasks fall away, new ones appear. But honesty plus involvement beats silence by a mile. Whoever says openly what's changing and lets people walk the path along keeps the team — instead of losing it.
