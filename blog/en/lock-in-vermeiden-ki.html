Avoiding lock-in: adopting AI without making yourself dependent

28 July 2026 · 2 min read · Maris Reinold

A new AI tool is quickly adopted — and quickly indispensable. That's convenient, until the vendor raises prices, discontinues the service, or holds your data in a format you can't get out of. Dependence creeps in quietly. You can avoid it with a few simple precautions.

Where lock-in comes from

Usually in three places: your data only lives in the vendor's format. Your processes are glued to a single interface. And the knowledge of how it all runs sits only inside that one tool. If the vendor goes, everything goes.

Three simple precautions

  • Your data stays your data. Make sure you can export it fully and in a common format at any time. No export, no trust.
  • Rely on open standards. Connections via open interfaces instead of proprietary special solutions are what make a later switch possible at all.
  • Keep the model swappable. If your process isn't nailed to a single AI model, you can move to a better or cheaper one without rebuilding everything.

Don't overshoot the other way

Just don't spend months building a perfectly vendor-neutral architecture while nothing usable emerges. A bit of attachment is perfectly fine — as long as, in a pinch, you can leave with your data and without drama. That's the actual point: not independence at any cost, but an open door.

Staying honest

No setup is a hundred percent switch-free. But the difference between “I can move in two weeks” and “I'm stuck” is decided at the start — in how you set up data and processes. Asking this question once at the outset costs almost nothing. Ignoring it later can get expensive.
