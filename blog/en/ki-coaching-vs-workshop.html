AI coaching vs. workshop: why the one-off training barely helps

24 July 2026 · 2 min read · Maris Reinold

You might know the feeling: an AI workshop, a full day, everyone's excited. At the end everyone nods, there's applause and good cheer. Two weeks later? Almost nobody uses it anymore. Why is that — and what actually helps?

Why the spark fizzles out

Knowledge you hear once but don't apply right away and repeatedly is quickly gone. After the workshop the daily grind returns — with full inboxes and the usual habits. The old routine almost always beats the good intention from training day.

What coaching does differently

Coaching isn't an event, it's accompaniment. You learn on the real, everyday tasks — not on examples from a slide. Over weeks, in small steps, in the middle of the work. That's how “shown once” gradually becomes a habit.

  • On the real case: not “here's how one could”, but “let's solve your task from today with it”.
  • Repeated: a move that was accompanied three times sticks. One that was demonstrated once doesn't.
  • Anchored in the team: the good tricks get written down so they don't vanish into one person's head.

How you notice the difference

There's a simple test: is the team still using the thing four weeks later — on its own, without a reminder? With a workshop the answer is often “no”. With good accompaniment, “yes”. The difference isn't the trainer's quality but the duration.

Staying honest

A workshop isn't useless. As a kickoff, to light the spark and take away the fear, it's good. Only as a standalone measure does it rarely change habits. The spark gets you moving — the accompaniment makes sure you don't come to a halt again.
