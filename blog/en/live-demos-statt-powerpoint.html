Try it yourself instead of PowerPoint: how live demos turn the AI debate

10 August 2026 · 2 min read · Maris Reinold

You know those AI meetings? Forty slides, nice graphics, big words. Everyone nods politely, nobody's really convinced. And then someone shows a small thing that works live and on real data — and suddenly the room turns.

Why slides fail with AI

AI stays abstract as long as you only describe it. Everyone in the room imagines something different — one a miracle cure, another a gimmick. Slides let those fantasies sit side by side instead of measuring them against reality.

What a demo changes

A demo makes it concrete: your data, your vocabulary, your problem. All at once the discussion gets specific. "Whether AI does anything at all" becomes "where exactly do we use this". Even the skeptics get specific — and that's exactly progress.

It doesn't have to be perfect

The trick: the demo has to be allowed to be small and rough. An ugly but working example convinces more than the prettiest slide deck. Hands-on over high-gloss — better something that runs than something that looks good.

Staying honest

A word of caution: a demo can also over-promise. The wow moment tempts you to hide the limits. Say openly what the thing can't do yet — that builds trust, not the effect alone.
