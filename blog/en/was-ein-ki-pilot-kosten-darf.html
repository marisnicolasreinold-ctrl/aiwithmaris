When an AI pilot is truly worth it

3 July 2026 · 2 min read · Maris Reinold

“Should we give this AI thing a try?” The question is quickly asked — and a pilot fizzles out just as quickly if no one says up front how you’ll know whether it’s any good. A pilot isn’t an end in itself. It’s a bet with a clear question: does this deliver real value here — yes or no?

For that bet to pay off it needs no grand undertaking, but three things: a goal, a deadline and an owner.

Start at the pain, not the tool

Before you start a pilot, name the problem it should solve — measurably. How much time does the task eat today? An example to follow along, the number is hypothetical: if two people each spend five hours a week on a dull task, that’s around 40 hours a month. The pilot may be measured against that number — not against a gut feeling.

A goal, a deadline, a name

The three most common reasons a pilot runs into the void: no measurable goal, no end date, no clear owner. Then it runs forever and never proves anything. The antidotes are simple — a number that should improve; a deadline by when; a name who takes care of it. That’s exactly the idea behind a time-boxed test: clarity in a few weeks instead of an open-ended project without end.

The effort no one plans for

  • Your time. A pilot needs support from the team — the hours for approvals and feedback are part of it.
  • The data base. If the data is a mess, cleaning it up is often the bigger chunk than the AI itself. Better to know beforehand.
  • Operation afterwards. A solution needs upkeep. “Build once and forget” doesn’t exist.

Keeping it small is an advantage, not a compromise

A good pilot is deliberately small: it should deliver an answer fast, not change the world. If the answer is yes, you have the basis for the next step. If it’s no, you’ve learned early and staked little — and move on to the next case. Either way it’s a win.

If an undertaking sounds more like a “major project” than a “first step”, it’s the wrong first step. The point of a pilot isn’t to be big right away, but to make clear quickly whether the big thing is worth it at all.
