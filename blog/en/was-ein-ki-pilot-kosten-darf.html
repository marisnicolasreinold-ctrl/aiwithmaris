What an AI pilot may cost — an honest calculation

3 July 2026 · 2 min read · Maris Reinold

“What's this AI thing going to cost us?” That question almost always comes first — understandable, but in this form unanswerable. It's like asking what a vehicle costs. Cargo bike or truck? For a pilot there's still an honest rule of thumb — and it doesn't start with the price but with the benefit.

The calculation starts at the pain, not the price tag

Before you ask what a pilot costs, put a number on what the problem costs you today. An example to follow along — the numbers are hypothetical, the approach isn't: if two employees each spend five hours a week on a dull task, that's around 40 working hours a month. You know what those cost. A pilot that saves a good chunk of them may be measured against that number — not against a gut feeling.

What a pilot may cost

The simple guardrail: a pilot may cost enough that you can absorb the loss if it fails — and little enough that you don't keep postponing it forever. For a clearly scoped first case in a smaller company, that usually means: a manageable one-off effort plus running costs for usage and operation. No corporate budget. If an offer sounds more like a “major project” than a “first step”, it's the wrong first step.

The costs no one puts on the invoice

  • Your time. A pilot needs support from the team — the hours for approvals and feedback belong in the calculation.
  • The data base. If the data is a mess, cleaning it up is often the bigger item than the AI itself. Better to know beforehand.
  • Operation afterwards. A solution needs upkeep. “Build once and forget” doesn't exist.

How to spot burning money

It gets expensive in the bad sense when three things come together: no measurable goal, no end date, no clear owner. Then a pilot runs forever, keeps costing and never proves anything. The antidotes are simple — a number, a deadline, a name. That's exactly the idea behind a time-boxed test: clarity in a few weeks, not an open-ended project without end.

The honest total

A good pilot isn't a cost risk but a limited bet with a clear payout: small stake, fast answer to the question “does this help?”. If the answer is yes, you have the basis for the next step. If it's no, you've lost little and learned a lot. Both are worth the money.
