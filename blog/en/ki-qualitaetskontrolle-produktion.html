AI in quality control: seeing the defects the eye misses

8 July 2026 · 2 min read · Maris Reinold

Visual quality control has a human problem: concentration wavers. At the start of the shift every glance lands; by the end every eye looks tired. A scratch, a dent, a misplaced part — across a thousand identical pieces, something eventually slips through. Not from carelessness, but because people aren't machines.

This is exactly where AI vision is strong. It doesn't tire, it inspects the thousandth part as precisely as the first — and spots patterns too subtle or too fast for the eye.

What AI does in inspection

  • Inspect evenly: every part by the same standard, no daily form. The hit rate doesn't drift with the time of day.
  • Spot fine deviations: hairline cracks, minimal colour differences, dimensional deviations in tenths of a millimetre — made visible before the part moves on.
  • From checking to preventing: if certain defects cluster, that's a signal — often the cause can be found in the process before the next scrap is produced.

What the human keeps

The AI flags, the human decides — especially in borderline cases. Is the small deviation a real defect or still within tolerance? That judgement, and the responsibility for it, stays with the experienced inspector. The AI takes the dull continuous checking off them and frees their mind for the tricky cases. “Look at everything” becomes “judge the flagged ones”.

The honest prerequisite

A vision AI has to learn what's good and what's bad — from real examples out of your production, including the rare defects. If there are barely any documented defect images, collecting them is the first step. And the camera and lighting setup has to be right; a blurry image blinds even the best AI.

Where to start

  • Which defect costs you most if it slips through to the customer?
  • Where does visual inspection tire fastest today?
  • Have you documented good and bad parts — or would that be the first step?
