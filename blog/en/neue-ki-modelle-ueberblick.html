New AI models every six months: which one fits which task?

15 July 2026 · 2 min read · Maris Reinold

Anyone following the AI news can barely keep up: every six months new model generations stand side by side — Claude at Anthropic, the GPT line at OpenAI, Gemini at Google. Each supposedly stronger than the last. For smaller companies, though, the interesting question isn’t “which is the best?” but “which fits which task?”.

The most common mistake: the strongest for everything

Many reflexively reach for the biggest, most capable model — for every task. That’s like making every trip by truck. For much of daily work — sorting, summarising, drafting standard texts — a fast, lean model is easily enough. The genuinely tricky thinking tasks get the strong one. Choose deliberately and you lose barely any quality while gaining speed.

Stronger doesn’t automatically mean better for you

A new top model is good news — but not every task needs the top. For smaller firms, progress shows less in there being an even cleverer model and more in the solid everyday models getting steadily better. Those do most of the real work.

What matters in practice

  • Don’t lock into one model. The landscape changes every six months. Solutions that allow a switch keep you agile — no lock-in.
  • Make usage visible. Modern platforms show what’s running and let you set which task uses which model. Use that instead of blindly running the strongest.
  • Choose by benefit, not by spec sheet. The question is never “which model wins the benchmark” but “which does my task reliably enough”.

Staying honest

The real work rarely sits in the model itself but in the clean integration, the data upkeep and the operation around it. The model is the engine, not the whole car. Keep that in view and you won’t be driven by the six-month churn of new names — you’ll calmly choose what fits the task.
