AI in customer service: what the assistant takes on — and where the human is needed

11 August 2026 · 2 min read · Maris Reinold

Customer service is a mountain of repetition — the same questions, again and again — with a few cases in between that really need a human. Exactly that sorting is something AI can take on. But only if you give it the right limits.

What AI takes on well

The standard questions: where's my order? What are the opening hours? How do returns work? An AI assistant can answer such enquiries instantly around the clock — and do the simple triage too, so the right thing lands with the right person.

Where the human is needed

Complaints, emotions, special cases, anything with money or law behind it. A bot simulating empathy while a customer is upset makes it worse, not better. Here the human isn't a luxury but a must.

The middle way

The best is rarely "all or nothing". AI as a first layer, with a clean handover to a human the moment it gets delicate. Or the AI writes the draft, the human proofreads and sends — especially for anything sensitive.

Staying honest

A bad chatbot is worse than none. Two rules help: measure it by whether it really helps — not by how many enquiries it "deflects". And be transparent that it's a bot, with a clear path to a human. Honesty beats the illusion of perfection.
