Business-driven AI: when the bottom line steers the project

22 June 2026 · 2 min read · Maris Reinold

“We're going to do something with AI now.” I hear that a lot — and it's usually the beginning of the end. It describes a tool in search of a problem. First the slick tool, then the question of what for. In the end a demo runs, everyone nods, and three months later no one uses it.

Business-driven means flipping the order. The technology doesn't drive the project — a number that hurts your business does. AI then isn't an end in itself but a means to an end, and the end is set in advance.

The difference in one sentence

Technology-driven asks: “What can we do with AI?” Business-driven asks: “Which problem costs us money, time or nerves — and is AI the best tool for it?” The first question leads to gimmicks. The second to projects that pay off.

Management doesn't care about model accuracy

A common mistake: measuring success technically. “The model recognises 94 percent of invoices correctly” — nice, but to management that means nothing. What they want to hear: “Invoice checking no longer takes three days, but one.” Or: “We're catching errors that used to slip past everyone.” Business-driven means defining success in the language of the business — in euros, hours, risk. Not in percentages of some technical term.

Every initiative gets exactly one number

Before you start, attach one metric to each AI case that should measurably improve. Not five — one. Turnaround time of a request. Hours per week on some grind. The rate of errors that slip through. If you can't name that number today, the case isn't ripe yet. Without a baseline you won't know in the end whether anything improved — or whether you just have a new tool in use.

Not every business problem is an AI problem

The uncomfortable truth: sometimes the honest answer is a clean process, a checklist or a better spreadsheet — not AI. Thinking business-driven means accepting that too. Start from the business question and you'll more often land at “we can solve this without AI in a week” — saving yourself a project that would never have paid off. That's not a failure. That's the point of the exercise.

How to do it concretely

  • Start at the pain, not the tool. Which process measurably costs the most?
  • Attach a number — and note today's value before you start.
  • Define success up front in business language: “from X to Y in Z weeks”.
  • Ask honestly whether AI is the best tool — or whether a simple fix will do.
  • Measure afterwards. Did the number move? If not, stop instead of spinning it.

Business-driven isn't a method for slide decks but an attitude: the technology serves, it doesn't show off. Start that way and you end up not with an AI demo but with a result that shows in the books.
