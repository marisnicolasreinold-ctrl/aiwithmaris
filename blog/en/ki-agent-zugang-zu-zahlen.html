What an AI agent with access to your numbers actually gets done

29 June 2026 · 2 min read · Maris Reinold

The last article was about MCP — the standard that lets an AI reach your real systems. Now it gets concrete. Because the question that really matters in a smaller company isn't “what's a protocol?”, but “what does the thing get done at my desk on a Tuesday morning?”

For that I work with Claude Code — an AI agent that doesn't just answer but acts: reads files, builds analyses, plugs into databases, spreadsheets and business tools via MCP. Not a chat window that gives advice, but a tool that takes on the task. Let's look at it through controlling.

A typical morning

Imagine the monthly numbers are loaded. Instead of clicking through the systems yourself, you give the agent a task in plain words:

  • “Pull the actuals and compare them to the plan.” The agent accesses the accounting data via MCP, computes the variances and puts the biggest outliers in front of you.
  • “Write me the commentary for management.” From the variances comes a draft in your tone — you add what only you know.
  • “Which open items are overdue?” The agent queries the database and delivers the list, sorted by amount and days.
  • “Build the liquidity outlook for the next eight weeks.” From actual payment behaviour rather than payment terms — and next time updated with a single sentence.

This isn't a vision of the future. It's possible today, because the agent can dock onto the systems where the numbers already live, via MCP.

Why “agent” is more than “chatbot”

A chatbot waits for questions and returns text. An agent breaks a task into steps, fetches the needed data itself, checks the interim result and carries on — until the task is done. The difference is between “explain how this works” and “do it and show me the result”. That leap is exactly what access via MCP makes possible.

The guardrails — it doesn't work without them

Power needs limits, especially with numbers. Three rules I stick to:

  • Read first, write later. In the first weeks the agent may only analyse, change nothing. You see how good it is without risk.
  • Narrow rights. Access to the one system the task needs — not to everything. Better to open five doors individually than hand over the master key.
  • A human approves. Anything that goes outside or feeds a decision gets proofread. The agent delivers the draft, the responsibility stays with you.

Honestly

This doesn't replace a controller. It takes the grind off them and frees time for the judgement. And it doesn't pay off everywhere: where the data is a mess, you have to tidy up first — no agent saves a broken data base. But where the numbers sit cleanly in systems and the same grind recurs every month, the leverage is large.

The core in one sentence: AI moves from advisor to colleague the moment it's safely allowed at your real systems — and MCP is the route there.
