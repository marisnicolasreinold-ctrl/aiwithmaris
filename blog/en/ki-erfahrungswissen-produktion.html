Securing hands-on knowledge: AI as the memory of your production

10 July 2026 · 2 min read · Maris Reinold

Every production floor has that one colleague. The one who hears at the machine that something's wrong before a gauge moves. Who knows why you throttle the feed rate with this material. Who knows the tricks that are in no manual. And who eventually retires.

Then the company faces a quiet problem: the most valuable knowledge of the production was never written down. It sat in one head — and walks out the door with it. Given the age structure in smaller companies, this isn't a one-off but a wave now rolling in in many places.

Where AI helps with knowledge

  • Gathering knowledge: instead of forcing a 200-page manual, you can collect experience in conversation — the AI asks follow-ups, summarises, organises. “Klaus knows that” becomes something findable.
  • Making knowledge accessible: the new colleague asks in plain words — “why does the line judder with material X?” — and gets the answer from the gathered experience, not from a folder no one opens.
  • Learning from incidents: what was done at the last similar failure? The AI finds the case again, instead of everyone reinventing the wheel.

The honest limit

An AI memory doesn't replace the experienced human — it preserves part of what they know and makes it shareable. The instinct, the in-the-moment intuition, can't be fully put into words. But much can: rules, causes, proven moves. Securing exactly that before it vanishes is the point.

And: willingness matters. Someone who fears making themselves redundant by sharing their knowledge won't share honestly. It helps to say openly what it's about — safeguarding the business, not replacing people.

Where to start

  • Whose departure would tear the biggest gap at your place?
  • Which knowledge sits in exactly one head today and nowhere else?
  • Where do new colleagues search longest for answers someone already knows?

Securing hands-on knowledge is rarely urgent — until it's too late. That's exactly why it's worth starting early. How to do it in practice is in the AI guide; for a look at your business, write me via contact.
