Why “we need an AI strategy first” often buries the project

7 August 2026 · 2 min read · Maris Reinold

"Before we do anything with AI, we need a strategy first." The sentence sounds sensible, grown-up, responsible. And quite often it's the place where the AI project gets quietly buried.

Why the grand strategy paralyses

What happens then: workshops, slides, responsibilities, a strategy paper. Months pass, but nothing runs. Not a single example anyone can touch. The initial enthusiasm fizzles, and in the end a document sits in a drawer that never became reality.

Strategy grows from experience

The thinking error: you can't design a good strategy for something you've never held in your hands. Whoever knows AI only from talks strategises over guesses. Only once you've solved a real case do you know what AI can really do in your business — and what it can't.

The reverse route

Flip it around: take a concrete, annoying problem. Solve it in weeks. Learn where AI holds up and where it jams. Exactly that knowledge is the raw material for a strategy worthy of the name — grounded instead of guessed.

Staying honest

That doesn't mean strategy is superfluous. It just belongs in the right place: after the first proof, not before. It's not about doing without, but about sequence. First a case that works — then the grand plan that grows out of it.
