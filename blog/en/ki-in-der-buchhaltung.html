AI in accounting: why the paper mountain is the perfect first use case

1 July 2026 · 2 min read · Maris Reinold

“Where should we actually start with AI?” I hear this question constantly — and my answer surprises many: look at accounting. Not because it's the most exciting place. But because almost everything that makes a good first use case comes together there.

Why accounting fits so well

  • It happens constantly. Invoices, receipts, bookings — every day, all year. AI pays off through repetition, and here there's repetition in abundance.
  • The rules are clear. What belongs on which account follows a logic. Where there are rules, AI can make suggestions that are largely right.
  • The benefit is measurable. Hours per week, days to payment, error rate — all countable. You see in black and white whether something improved.
  • A mistake is cheap to catch. A wrong booking suggestion is checked and corrected before it's posted. No drama, no customer sees it.

What the AI actually takes over

Picture the typical flow: receipts arrive by email, as PDF, as a photo. Today someone types the data, assigns it, ticks it off against the order. With AI the system reads the receipts, proposes the account assignment, matches invoice and order, and only puts forward the cases where something doesn't fit. “All by hand” becomes “just check the exceptions”.

The honest prerequisite

For this to work, receipts have to arrive digital and reasonably ordered. If half of them sit as crumpled paper slips in a drawer, the first project isn't the AI but the clean intake. That's not bad news — that step pays off anyway, with or without AI.

And: the AI doesn't book on its own. It suggests, a human approves — especially at the start. That way you see how good the suggestions are before you hand over responsibility. Category by category, not everything at once.

Where to start

  • Which receipt type comes most often and is most uniform?
  • How many hours per week go into pure typing today?
  • Do the receipts already arrive digital — or is that the real first step?

Accounting is rarely anyone's favourite topic — but that's exactly why it's a rewarding one. Here AI proves its value in weeks, not years, and builds the trust for the next step.
