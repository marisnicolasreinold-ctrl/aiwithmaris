AI in production planning: when the plan reshuffles itself

9 July 2026 · 2 min read · Maris Reinold

The production plan is done in the morning — and outdated by noon. A rush order comes in, a shift calls in sick, a machine acts up. Then someone sits and shuffles jobs back and forth, makes calls, works out in their head which order hurts least. This constant replanning eats time — and often rests more on experience than on a clean calculation.

Here AI helps not as magic but as a fast number-cruncher: it checks in seconds what a human plays through in half an hour.

Where AI comes in for fine planning

  • Optimising sequences: which order minimises setup times, holds deadlines and loads the machines evenly? The AI works through many variants, not just the two you manage by hand.
  • Cushioning disruptions: if a machine fails, it immediately proposes a new plan — instead of replanning blocking an hour.
  • What-if in minutes: “If we pull the rush order forward, what does that cost the other deadlines?” An answer with numbers, not gut feeling.

The difference: thinking along instead of planning once

The real gain isn't the perfect morning plan but the ongoing adjustment. A plan that recalculates itself at every disruption isn't right at eight o'clock but all day. The planner doesn't lose control — they get a proposal they can accept, change or reject.

Staying honest

The AI knows the formal rules but not every quiet arrangement on the floor — that this machine is better run by the experienced colleague, that this customer tolerates no delay. Such soft factors stay in human hands. And without reliable master data — setup times, capacities, availabilities — even AI plans into the blue. Data base first, then optimisation.

Where to start

  • How much time goes into replanning at your place every day?
  • Which disruption throws the plan off most often?
  • Are setup times and capacities cleanly recorded — or in the heads of a few?

Even a plan that delivers a good proposal within minutes of a disruption takes pressure out of the day.
